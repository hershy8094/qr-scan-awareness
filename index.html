Relax — this one really is a demo

This page reads all of the above only to show it back to you, right here on your screen. It keeps no log and sends nothing to us. The one outside request it makes is a lookup of your IP's rough location through a public service — the same thing any website can do the instant you land on it. The one thing it keeps is a random ID in your browser's local storage, which you can clear in your browser settings. Trackers run the same idea across thousands of sites at once. The point is simply to show how much a page you never chose can learn in a heartbeat.

Why it matters

A QR code is just a link you can't read

01

You can't see where it goes

A written link gives you clues. A QR code hides the address inside a pattern, so "menu" or "free parking" can point anywhere at all.

02

Stickers are easy to swap

Scammers paste their own code over the real one on parking meters, posters and payment signs. The sticker looks official; the destination isn't.

03

The page loads before you think

By the time you read the address bar, the site has your full profile — and a convincing fake login is one tap from your password.

Before you scan

Five habits that keep you safe

01

Check the real address first. Your phone previews the link before opening it. Read it. If it isn't the site you expect, close it.

02

Be suspicious of stickers. Look for a code pasted on top of another, or one that seems added as an afterthought. When in doubt, type the web address yourself.

03

Never enter a password or payment from a scan. If a scanned page asks you to log in or pay, open your banking or account app directly instead.

04

Watch for urgency. "Your account is locked", "pay now to avoid a fine" — pressure is the oldest trick for switching off your judgement.

05

Keep your phone and browser updated. A modern browser hands over less and blocks more. Updates close the holes a malicious page tries to use once you land on it.

Lock it down

Make your browser harder to read

You can't become invisible online, but you can stop the cheap tricks from working. Most of these take a few minutes and cost nothing.

Browsers worth trying

  • Firefox: set Enhanced Tracking Protection to Strict. Also available on Android.
  • Brave: blocks trackers and ads, and scrambles canvas and audio data.
  • Tor Browser: the strongest option. It tries to make every user look the same. Slower, so keep it for sensitive browsing.
  • DuckDuckGo (mobile): blocks trackers and upgrades links to HTTPS.
  • Safari (iPhone): Intelligent Tracking Prevention is on by default.

Extensions, kept short

  • uBlock Origin: blocks ads, trackers and many malicious scripts.
  • Privacy Badger: learns which trackers to block, with no list to maintain.
  • Every extension adds to what a site can see about you, so fewer is better. Skip "free VPN" and "anonymity" add-ons. Some sell browsing data.

Settings to change today

  • HTTPS-only mode. Firefox: Privacy & Security. Chrome: Settings, Security, Always use secure connections.
  • Block third-party cookies. Stops sites following you from one to another.
  • Ask where to save each file. Chrome: Settings, Downloads. You see every download first.
  • Site permissions. Set location and camera to "ask" or off for sites you don't trust.
  • A password manager. It won't autofill on a lookalike domain, which is how many QR and phishing pages collect passwords.
  • Phone basics. Passcode on, auto-updates on, two-factor on your email and bank.

Check yourself

  • EFF Cover Your Tracks: how unique your browser looks.
  • BrowserLeaks: detailed canvas, WebRTC and font checks.
  • AmIUnique: how rare your fingerprint is.

Run one check now, make the changes, then run it again. A VPN hides your IP from sites but not your device fingerprint, so treat it as one layer.
